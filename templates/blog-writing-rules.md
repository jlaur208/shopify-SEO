# Blog writing rules

Read before drafting or rewriting any Curiosity Corner article. Built 2026-10-05 from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" project page, Kobak et al. (arXiv 2406.07016), the FSU "delve" study, The Atlantic and Marketplace on negative parallelism, the Rick Steves AI-vs-human test, Nieman Lab on BuzzFeed's AI travel articles, Paul Graham's "Writes and Write-Nots", and Google's spam policies and helpful-content guidance. Jared's own rules (full sentences, no em dashes, no label-then-list, no closers, no antithesis, no triplets) sit on top of these.

## Words and phrases that do not appear

Verbs: delve, embark, elevate, enhance, foster, cultivate, showcase, underscore, highlight, emphasize, garner, bolster, navigate (figurative), unlock, unleash, leverage, streamline, boast, and "stands as / serves as / marks / represents" where "is" will do, or "features / offers / maintains" where "has" will do.

Adjectives: vibrant, bustling, breathtaking, unforgettable, stunning, seamless, robust, crucial, pivotal, vital, key, intricate, meticulous, comprehensive, enduring, profound, rich, renowned, groundbreaking, diverse, dynamic, ever-evolving, picturesque, quaint, charming, iconic, world-class.

Nouns: tapestry, testament, realm, landscape (figurative), journey, interplay, insights, legacy, hub, gem, treasure trove, array, oasis, haven, paradise, getaway, and "adventure" as filler.

Phrases: hidden gem, nestled in, in the heart of, whether you're a ... or a ..., look no further, in today's, it's important to note, it's worth noting, in conclusion, overall, ultimately, at the end of the day, must-visit, must-see, something for everyone, memories that last a lifetime, next level, rich history, natural beauty, plays a crucial role, sets the stage, paves the way, a wide range of, now I know what you're thinking, and any unsourced "experts say", "locals say", "visitors rave".

## Sentence patterns that do not appear

Negative parallelism ("it's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y"). Rule of three in adjectives, clauses, or bullets. "From X to Y" sweeps. Rhetorical questions opening a section. Sentences that announce the next sentence ("Here's what you need to know"). Sentence-final participles ("..., ensuring a memorable stay"). Em dashes. Exclamation points. Hedge stacks ("may potentially", "generally"). Colon-list setups ("offers three advantages:"). Sentences starting with Additionally, Moreover, Furthermore. Closers that summarize or beg ("Book today!").

## Structures that do not appear

A self-describing intro ("In this guide, we'll explore"). Bold lead-ins on bullets. Generic H2s (Introduction, Overview, Why Visit, Final Thoughts, Conclusion, FAQ). Emoji in headings. Sections of equal length that each end with a one-line summary. An FAQ dump at the bottom. Bold mid-sentence. Tables for things that belong in a sentence. Bullets where prose would do. Anything that could be pasted onto another brand unchanged.

## What a post does instead

The first paragraph answers the question the reader searched. Then the detail. Every section carries a number, a name, or a direction: an age range, a product name, a developmental milestone, a safety standard, a study. Caveats are stated plainly: what is closed in winter, where the cell signal drops, what to skip. The brand's own vantage point appears where it is real: what parents ask customer service, what a product does at a given age. Comparisons name the alternative and give the reason to pick one. Copulas are plain ("is", "has"). Sentence lengths vary. One idea per paragraph. If nothing is decided in a paragraph, delete it. Every claim traces to babyeinstein.com, the Shopify product data, the intake file, or a primary source (AAP, CPSC, a published study) that the draft notes record.

## Google

Google says the question is content quality, not how it was produced (developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content). Scaled content abuse is many pages made to rank rather than to help, however they are made (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). The test is whether a reader would bookmark or recommend the page and whether the sourcing and expertise are visible. Bylines are real people; no invented author profiles.
